# Candidate–job relevance rubric (lab, 25 Nov 2026)

You are judging whether a real job posting is a sensible next job for one candidate, from the candidate's résumé and
the candidate's stated direction (shown with each candidate; it is what they told us they want next — it is a
preference, NOT evidence of experience).

Grade each pair with exactly one of:
- "direct": a suitable direct fit. The same kind of work the résumé shows, at a level they would realistically be hired
  at, the main requirements visible in the résumé, and in line with their stated direction.
- "adjacent": a suitable adjacent fit. Neighbouring work or domain that the résumé's experience genuinely transfers to,
  consistent with their direction (or their stated openness to related work); a fair recommendation.
- "stretch": a stretch or uncertain fit. Plausible, but with a real doubt: a level step up, an important requirement not
  shown, a domain shift, or a posting too thin to judge.
- "unsuitable": a different kind of work, clearly the wrong level, or key requirements absent.
- "invalid": not a real job posting a person can act on (navigation-only page, empty, landing page, test), or the person
  is plainly ineligible under a mandatory constraint stated in the posting (e.g. a licence they do not hold, a language).

Also give:
- "same_work": true/false — is it the same kind of work the person has actually done (or directly beside it)? Sharing
  vocabulary is not enough. Managing a team that does X is not doing X; supporting an outcome is not owning it.
- "level": "below" | "right" | "one_up" | "too_far".
- "missing": up to 3 short items the posting requires that the résumé does not show (empty if none).
- "reason": at most 20 words, the deciding reason. No personal details.

Be strict and honest. Do not be generous because a title sounds similar; read what the posting asks. Do not reject a
role only because the posting is written in general terms.
